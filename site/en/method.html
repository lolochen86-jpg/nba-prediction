Method & sources

Update pipeline: fetch rosters → reconcile injuries → update per-36 statistics → allocate minutes → estimate scores → simulate win probabilities and scoring ranges → generate pages → deploy.

Prediction pipeline

  1. Available roster — ESPN team rosters include experience, position and headshots; only available players enter the lineup.
  2. Injury adjustments — Injury reports from ESPN, CBS and Yahoo are compared; the most restrictive reported status determines availability: Out / Suspended → 0 minutes、Doubtful → 15%、Questionable → 50%、 Day-to-day → 75%、Probable → 95%. Conflicting reports are flagged on the game card with ⚠︎。
  3. Player strength — Current-season Basketball-Reference per-36 statistics are shrunk toward previous-season values (k=12 games) to reduce early-season small-sample volatility. Missing current players are supplemented with current-season ESPN regular-season logs.
  4. Minutes allocation — Allocate 240 team minutes using potential output per minute and scoring weights, capped at 40 minutes per player. Unavailable minutes shift to replacements, including their lower projected efficiency.
  5. Team efficiency — Available-lineup scoring divided by full-lineup scoring produces an availability index. Adjusted offensive efficiency is geometrically blended with opposing defensive efficiency (0.58 / 0.42) to estimate points per possession.
  6. Game prediction — Expected possessions × points per possession gives expected points, with a ±1.4-point home-court adjustment.
  7. Uncertainty — σ = 10.3 points plus injury uncertainty and an early-season small-sample penalty, capped at 15 points.
  8. Monte Carlo simulation — 20,000 simulations produce straight-up win probabilities, five-point scoring-range probabilities and the most likely score.

Reading scoring ranges

Scoring ranges use five-point intervals: 101–105, 106–110, 111–115, etc. Each card shows the four most likely ranges for each team. Hitting both teams' ranges is less likely than hitting one; backtesting reports these separately.

Known limitations

  • No betting lines or odds are used; predictions are based on lineups and efficiency.
  • Back-to-back games, travel and unannounced load management are not modeled.
  • Samples are small during the first 10 games; uncertainty increases and confidence falls.
  • Where current data are unavailable, traded or two-way players may use prior-season statistics or league-average estimates.
